"Business Model" — what they actually mean

In everyday language, a business model just answers one question:
"How does this solution actually work and survive in the real world?" 
Not just "what's the cool idea," but "who does it help, 
who pays for it (if anyone), how does it get built and kept running,
 and why would it keep existing after the hackathon ends?"

Usually want you to cover things like:

The problem — stated clearly and briefly (you've already got this)
The solution — what you're building/offering
Who it's for — who uses it (government dept, NGO, schools, farmers, etc.) — your "customer" or "beneficiary"
How it delivers value — why is this better than what exists now
How it's funded or sustained — is it free and government-adopted, does an NGO fund it, is there a subscription, grant funding, etc.
How it scales — could this work in one province and then expand nationally?
Team/resources — what's needed to actually build and run it (data access, partnerships, tech)


The business model — filled in with actual reasoning, not template answers
Problem: ~40% of South African learners who start Grade 1 never finish matric; existing early-warning tools are manual, spreadsheet-based, and not scaled nationally.
Solution: A trained early-warning model (logistic regression / random forest) that scores individual learners or schools by dropout risk using academic, behavioral, and attendance-style features, feeding a dashboard that ranks who needs help first.
Who it's for: Not the learner directly — the user is the school/district official or NGO caseworker who acts on the flag. The learner is the beneficiary.
Value over what exists: Replaces a static, rule-based Excel traffic light with a model that can use more features and update as new data comes in — more accurate targeting of scarce support resources.
How it's sustained: Doesn't need to invent a funding model from scratch — positions itself as a tool that plugs into an already-funded ecosystem (DGMT/Zero Dropout Campaign, provincial education departments) rather than needing learners or schools to pay anything.
How it scales: Pilot in one province/district with existing NGO partners → validate against real outcomes → propose integration with SA-SAMS/LURITS, the exact national system the campaign already asked DBE to build.
What's needed to build it: access to historical enrollment/attendance/performance data (GHS, DBE SNAP, or a partner NGO's own records), and ideally a relationship with one of these existing organizations rather than starting in isolation.
