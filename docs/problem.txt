EARLY WARNING SYSTEM FOR SCHOOL DROPOUT (SOUTH AFRICA)
Hackathon Submission — Problem, Solution & Business Model
=========================================================
(Format matches the required submission: Problem, Solution, Business Model.
 Pair this with the PowerPoint and the technical plan in plan_v1.txt.)


1. THE PROBLEM
---------------------------------------------------------
About 40% of learners who start Grade 1 in South Africa never
make it to Grade 12 — they disappear from the school system
somewhere along the way.
(Source: DG Murray Trust's Zero Dropout Campaign)

Meaning: out of every 100 kids who start Grade 1, about 40 are
gone before matric.

Research points to Grade 10, 11, and 12 as the years where the
steepest drop-off happens (nationally, only ~84% move from
Grade 10 to 11, and ~78% move from Grade 11 to 12). We are
citing this existing research, not claiming we discovered it —
we have not yet independently proven this number from our own
data.

The real gap: there is no widely deployed, data-driven tool
that predicts, ahead of time, WHICH schools are at highest risk
of losing learners — so districts and NGOs currently find out
mostly after the fact, when it's too late to help.

Important: this is NOT about matric pass rates or exam results.
We are not disputing Umalusi or DBE's numbers. Pass rate and
dropout rate measure two different things. Our focus is only on
learners leaving the system before they even get to write the
exam.


2. OUR SOLUTION
---------------------------------------------------------
A model that predicts, per school, the expected dropout % for
its current Grade 10, 11, and 12 cohorts — using a school's own
history (enrollment trends, funding level, teacher-to-learner
ratio, past pass-rate trend) — so districts and support
organizations know WHICH schools to help FIRST, BEFORE learners
actually leave.

Example output:
  "School X is predicted to lose 25% of its Grade 10 class
   before reaching matric."

In one line:
"We're not trying to fix the whole education system. We're
trying to give people a warning light — so they know WHICH
schools to help first, and BEFORE the damage is already done."


3. WHY THIS APPROACH
---------------------------------------------------------
- Grade 10-12 is where research shows the steepest drop, so
  that's where our model focuses.
- We predict at the SCHOOL level, not the individual learner
  level, because individual learner records are private and
  protected by law (POPIA) — we don't have legal access to
  named student data. School-level data is public.
- The output is a PERCENTAGE per school, not a yes/no per
  student — this makes it a resource-ranking tool for
  decision-makers, not a surveillance tool on individual kids.
- This isn't a made-up idea. The Zero Dropout Campaign already
  told the Department of Basic Education that an Early Warning
  System is needed, and already runs a manual, spreadsheet-
  based, individual-level version inside specific schools (a
  red/amber/green "traffic light" system). Our school/district-
  level model is the missing macro layer above that — it tells
  a province WHICH schools to prioritise, not just which
  individual kid inside one school to watch.


4. BUSINESS MODEL
---------------------------------------------------------

4.1 THE PROBLEM (brief)
  ~40% of learners who start Grade 1 never finish Grade 12,
  worst between Grade 10-12. No existing open, data-driven tool
  predicts ahead of time WHICH schools are at highest risk, so
  help can be sent before it's needed rather than after learners
  have already left.

4.2 THE SOLUTION
  A model that predicts each school's expected dropout % for its
  Grade 10-12 cohorts, using open historical school data
  (enrollment trends, funding quintile, teacher-learner ratio,
  pass-rate trend), and ranks schools/districts so decision-
  makers know where to act first — delivered as a live Grist
  database feeding a Flourish dashboard.

4.3 WHO IT'S FOR
  USERS (who operate the tool):
    - Department of Basic Education (national) and provincial
      education departments — resource allocation, and finally
      tracking dropout as a measurable KPI
    - NGOs already in this exact space (Zero Dropout Campaign,
      Masibumbane Development Organisation) — a scaled-up
      replacement for their manual Excel traffic-light system
    - District officials and school principals — a ranked,
      actionable list instead of guesswork
  BENEFICIARIES (who the value ultimately reaches):
    - The learners and families who get support before dropping
      out, instead of being counted only afterward

4.4 HOW IT DELIVERS VALUE
    - Existing tool: a manual Excel spreadsheet with a simple
      red/amber/green rule-of-thumb, run school-by-school, by
      whichever NGO happens to be present in that school.
    - Our tool: a trained statistical model, run at district/
      province scale, using multiple real factors at once (not
      just one rule), so it can rank ALL schools in a province
      against each other — something no one currently has a way
      to do.
    - It turns "we think dropout is a problem here" into "this
      specific school is projected to lose 25% of its Grade 10
      class, and here's the main factor driving that number" —
      which is what actually justifies where money and staff get
      sent.

4.5 HOW IT'S FUNDED / SUSTAINED
    - Not sold to learners, families, or schools — it is a tool
      FOR government/NGOs, not a product they buy from us.
    - It plugs into an ecosystem that already funds this exact
      problem: DG Murray Trust funds the Zero Dropout Campaign
      already; provincial education departments already have
      EMIS/data budgets.
    - Realistic funding paths: (a) grant funding from an existing
      education-focused funder (DGMT or similar), (b) adoption/
      commissioning by a provincial education department as a
      planning tool, (c) partnership with an NGO that absorbs it
      into their existing program funding.
    - It does not need a subscription model to survive — its
      sustainability comes from being useful enough that an
      existing funded body wants to keep running it.

4.6 HOW IT SCALES
    Step 1: Pilot in ONE province with the most complete open
    data — Western Cape (confirmed strongest during our own data
    collection: see plan_v1.txt for exact sources).
    Step 2: Validate predictions against that province's own real
    historical outcomes (does a school we flagged as high-risk
    actually show a bigger drop the following year?).
    Step 3: Expand province by province, re-checking that the
    same features still predict well in each new province
    (funding/context differs a lot between provinces).
    Step 4 (long-term/national): propose integration with
    SA-SAMS and LURITS — the exact national learner-tracking
    system DBE has already been asked to build by the Zero
    Dropout Campaign — so the model eventually runs on live,
    national data instead of separately-collected provincial
    files.

4.7 TEAM / RESOURCES
    DATA ACCESS:
      - Open datasets already secured for the Western Cape pilot
        (see plan_v1.txt, Section 1 — Data Inventory)
      - DataFirst account (free registration) for the remaining
        gated datasets (national EMIS masterlist, SNAP survey,
        NSC microdata)
    PARTNERSHIPS:
      - A conversation with the Zero Dropout Campaign / DGMT
        would be extremely valuable — they already have real
        outcome data and credibility with DBE, and could sanity-
        check our model against ground truth
    PEOPLE / SKILLS NEEDED ON THE TEAM:
      - Someone to collect and clean the data (joining tables by
        EMIS number, extracting tables from PDFs, handling
        missing years)
      - Someone to build and test the model (Python — pandas +
        scikit-learn for linear regression / random forest /
        gradient boosting)
      - Someone to build the presentation layer (Grist for the
        live data table, Flourish for the map/chart dashboard)
      - Someone to handle the pitch/business case (this document,
        the "why this matters" framing for judges)
    TECH STACK (all free/low-cost, usable within a hackathon):
      - Python (pandas, scikit-learn) for modelling
      - Grist for the structured, shareable database
      - Flourish for the public-facing visualization
      - No paid infrastructure required to build a working
        prototype


5. IMPORTANT LIMITATIONS TO BE HONEST ABOUT (say this to judges)
---------------------------------------------------------
- We predict at the SCHOOL level, not the individual learner
  level — we don't have access to private, named student data.
- Some confirmed public datasets (SNAP survey, NSC microdata)
  are only clearly open up to around 2016 without registration;
  recent years need PDF-sourced data or a DataFirst account —
  this is real, planned-for manual work, not an oversight.
- The model won't be perfect:
    - False positive: a flagged school doesn't lose as many
      learners as predicted — low cost, just extra support sent
      that wasn't strictly needed.
    - False negative: an unflagged school still loses learners we
      didn't predict — the real risk, and why the model needs
      regular retraining and monitoring.
- "Grade 10-12 is the steepest" is based on existing external
  research, not a number we've independently proven from our own
  data yet — our model, once built, is what lets us check this
  claim against real numbers.
- Given the hackathon timeframe, our first working version is a
  risk-ranking MVP built from the open Western Cape data we could
  access directly (see plan_v1.txt) — the fully validated,
  cohort-tracked regression described above is the target
  architecture, with a clear roadmap to get there as gated
  datasets (SNAP, national EMIS, NSC microdata) become available.
